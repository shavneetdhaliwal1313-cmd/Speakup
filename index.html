CORE STANDARD

Sound Human, Not AI

This program is not about sounding impressive. It is about sounding like a real, thoughtful person — natural, clear, confident and professional.

AI / textbook

“I am writing to follow up regarding the matter referenced below. Please advise if you have had an opportunity to review the information provided.”

Natural human

“Just following up on the email below. Have you had a chance to review it?”

Too formal

“I would appreciate it if you could kindly provide me with an update at your earliest convenience.”

Real workplace English

“Just checking in to see if there’s any update.”

Everyday rule: Correct ≠ natural. Fancy ≠ professional. Short ≠ rude. Human + clear + appropriate = strong communication.

Negotiation Mastery

Negotiation Lab — expert study + deliberate practice

Watch actively, extract one technique, practise it aloud, then apply it to a realistic workplace situation.

🎓 Expert foundations

  • William Ury / Harvard PON: separate people from the problem, focus on interests rather than positions, create options for mutual gain and use objective standards. Harvard study video
  • Chris Voss / Black Swan: tactical empathy, calibrated questions, active listening, labels and mirrors. How To Negotiate
  • William Ury: pause and “go to the balcony” rather than react emotionally. Sustainable Agreements
  • Alexandra Carter: powerful questions, asking for more, handling “no,” and difficult conversations. Expert courses

🧠 Core concepts

BATNA

Best alternative if no agreement is reached.

Reservation point

The point beyond which accepting a deal is worse than your alternative.

Interests vs. positions

Discover why someone wants something instead of arguing only about the demand.

Anchoring

A first meaningful proposal can influence the negotiation range.

Objective criteria

Use legitimate standards, data, precedent or benchmarks.

Calibrated questions

Questions that make the other side think through the problem.

Silence

Do not rush to fill every pause.

Positive No

Protect your interest while preserving the relationship.

🥋 Practice drills

Interest discovery: Someone rejects your proposal. Ask three questions that uncover the reason behind the position.

Label: Start with “It sounds like…” and accurately describe the other person's concern.

Calibrated question: Replace “Can you do this?” with a question beginning with How or What.

Positive No: Say no to an unreasonable request while preserving the relationship.

Silence: State your proposal once, then practise staying silent for five seconds.

BATNA: Write your ideal outcome, acceptable outcome, BATNA and walk-away point.

Legal workplace: A counsel, adjuster, client or coworker pushes back on timing, scope, settlement or next steps. Negotiate without becoming defensive.

Read Like a Communicator

Short public-domain novel excerpts, followed by comprehension, vocabulary, tone and “say it naturally” practice. The goal is to learn how strong writers create rhythm, clarity and human voice.
